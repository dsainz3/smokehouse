# Smoked Pulled Pork

## Context

- Protein: Pork
- Cut / Size: Bone-in pork shoulder (Boston butt), 8–10 lbs
- Smoker / Grill: Pit Boss Austin XL
- Cook Style: Smoke + Wrap
- Wood (if smoking): Hickory, or hickory + apple blend
- Author:
- Notes: Cook to probe-tenderness, not temp. The stall is real — trust the process.

---

## Ingredients

### Rub (makes enough for one 8–10 lb butt)

- 3 tbsp coarse kosher salt
- 2 tbsp black pepper (coarse ground)
- 2 tbsp smoked paprika
- 1 tbsp garlic powder
- 1 tbsp onion powder
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tsp cayenne pepper (adjust to heat preference)
- 1 tsp dry mustard powder
- 1 tsp celery salt

### Everything else

- Pork shoulder / Boston butt (bone-in, 8–10 lbs)
- Yellow mustard (binder)
- Spritz: **50% apple cider vinegar + 50% apple juice**
- Wrap liquid: ¼ cup apple juice + 2 tbsp butter
- Optional: BBQ sauce (finish/serving)

---

## Prep

- Trim excess fat cap to ~¼ inch — enough for basting, not so thick it won't render
- Pat dry
- Apply thin, even coat of yellow mustard as binder
- Apply rub generously on all sides, pressing in; don't be shy on the fat cap
- Wrap and refrigerate overnight (8–12 hours), or rest 30–60 minutes at room temp while
  smoker preheats

---

## Cook

### Setup

- Temp: **225–250°F**
- Zone placement: fat cap up, thick side toward the hotter right side
- Pan / Grate: on grates over a drip pan

### Timing

1. **Smoke phase (5–8 hours unwrapped):** smoke at 225–250°F until bark is set and deep
   mahogany — typically around **165–170°F internal**
   - Spritz every 60–90 minutes after the first 3 hours (once bark starts setting)
2. **Wrap phase:** wrap tight in heavy-duty foil with ¼ cup apple juice and 2 tbsp butter;
   return to smoker at **250°F** until probe-tender

### Target Temps

- Wrap: **~165–170°F** (bark set, color deep)
- Pull: **~200–205°F** — but use the probe test: thermometer should slide in with zero
  resistance in multiple spots
- Estimated total time: **10–14 hours** depending on size; plan for variance

---

## Finish (Optional)

- Pull by hand or bear claws once rested; discard or chop any rubbery fat
- Mix a splash of collected drip pan juices back into the pulled pork to restore moisture
- Hot finish / glaze: optional light coat of sauce, tossed in

---

## Rest

- Duration: **1–2 hours minimum**
- Method: double-wrap in foil + towel in a dry cooler; carryover and juice redistribution
  are critical for moisture

## Lessons Learned

- Note ambient temp and whether you kept the lid shut — every peek adds time
