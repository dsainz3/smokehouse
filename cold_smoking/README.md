# Cold Smoking

Cold smoking exposes food to smoke at temperatures below **90°F** (ideally 68–86°F), adding
smoke flavor without cooking the food. It is used for curing, preserving, and flavoring foods
that are either already safe to eat (cheese, salt) or will be cooked separately (salmon, bacon).

> **Food safety note:** Cold smoking does not cook food. Proteins cold-smoked for eating
> raw (e.g. salmon, fish) must be properly cured first to inhibit bacterial growth.
> When in doubt, cure before smoking and refrigerate immediately after.

## Key Differences from Hot Smoking

| | Hot Smoking | Cold Smoking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temp range | 225–275°F | Below 90°F (ideally 68–86°F) |
| Cooks the food? | Yes | No |
| Duration | Hours | Hours to days |
| Primary purpose | Cook + flavor | Flavor + preserve |
| Requires cure? | No | Often yes (proteins) |
